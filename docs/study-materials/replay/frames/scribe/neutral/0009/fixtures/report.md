## Summary

The survey covered four hundred kilometres of shoreline between March and September. Rates of retreat were higher than the 2019 baseline at every site except Ardmore, where a new revetment has held the line.

Measurements were taken with the same photogrammetric method used in 2019, so the two are directly comparable.[^1]

## Method

### Sites

Sites were chosen to repeat the 2019 sample. Two were dropped because access is no longer safe.

- Ardmore, revetted in 2021
- Blackrock, unprotected
- Carrig Head, partially protected

### Instruments

A fixed-wing drone flew each site at ninety metres. Ground control points were re-surveyed at the start of each visit, because settlement had moved two of the 2019 markers.

Cloud cover forced three re-flights. Where a re-flight was more than a fortnight after the first attempt, both were kept and the earlier one marked provisional.[^2]

## Results

### Retreat rates

Mean retreat across the unprotected sites was 0.8 metres per year, against 0.5 in the 2019 baseline. The distribution is skewed: two sites account for most of the increase.

The 2019 report was careful about this:

Retreat is not steady. A cliff can stand for a decade and then lose four metres in one winter, and a mean taken over

too few years will read that winter as a trend.

We have kept the same ten-year window for that reason.

### Ardmore

Ardmore retreated 0.1 metres per year, within measurement error of no change at all. The revetment appears to be working, though it is too early to say whether it has moved the problem along the coast rather than solved it.

## Recommendations

- Repeat the survey in 2029
- Add two sites east of Ardmore
- Publish the point clouds, not only the summaries

The cost of the 2029 survey is estimated at forty thousand, assuming the same method and no additional sites.

[^1]: The 2019 report describes the method in full.

[^2]: Provisional flights are excluded from the means in section 3.
