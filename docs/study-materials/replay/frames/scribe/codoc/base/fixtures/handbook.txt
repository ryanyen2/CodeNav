3. Working alone

3.1 Before you go

3.1.1 Tell somebody

Leave your route and your expected return with the duty officer. This
is the single measure that most reduces the consequences of anything
else going wrong, and it costs a phone call.

3.1.2 Check the tide

The tide table on the office wall is authoritative. Phone applications
have been wrong twice, both times by an hour, both times because they
were still on winter time.

3.1.3 Equipment

• A charged radio, not only a phone
• Waterproof notebook
• Whistle

3.1.4 What counts as working alone

You are working alone if nobody within sight or hearing knows what
you are doing. Two people at opposite ends of a beach are both work-
ing alone.

3.2 On site

Keep the radio on. If you change your plan, say so on the radio; the
duty officer cannot help with a plan they do not know about.

The following are not permitted when working alone, in any conditions:

1. Entering water above the knee, whether or not you are wearing a
   buoyancy aid and whether or not the water appears calm.
2. Climbing above two metres.
3. Working after dark without a second radio.

3.3 Coming back

Call in when you are off site, not when you get home. The difference
has mattered once.

3.4 If somebody does not call in

The duty officer waits thirty minutes, then calls. If there is no
answer after two attempts, they call the coastguard. They do not
drive out to look, because a second person out of contact is worse
than the first.
