## How to group

1. Aim for 3 to 8 nodes at the top level after grouping, and no more than about 12 features under one theme. A theme that would hold more is two themes: split it by stage, or by what a caller uses apart from the layer under it.
2. One new level only: themes on top, existing features directly under them.
3. Group by stage of the work ("Preparing a request", "Sending and retrying"), not by kind of thing ("Utilities", "Models", "Helpers", "Types").
   - The call paths decide this, ahead of the coupling counts. Coupling says two things touch often. A path says they are steps of one job, in this order.
4. A feature that appears early in several paths is an entry point. Put its theme near the top.
5. Package identity, version constants and build configuration go last.
6. A feature that stands alone stays at the top level only when a reader would look for it by its own name. A small helper or shared type goes under the theme whose features use it most (the coupling counts say which). Deprecated names and compatibility shims go together under one theme, after the rest.
7. A theme's title names what its features do together. It is never the title of a feature under it.

## Fields

- A theme: `add_node` with a temporary id (`"id": "t1"`), a `title`, and a `description`. The description is one sentence that names, in backticks, the one or two symbols a caller enters through and what they return or change. Add a numbered list of stages only when a call path above visits its features in that order; a list of the child titles is not stages.
- A move: `move_node` with the feature's real id (`b-…`) as `feature_id` and the theme's temporary id as `parent_id`.
- A feature that stays at the top level: `move_node` with no `parent_id`.

## Order

The ops are the top level in reading order (rules 4 and 5). Name every existing
top-level feature once:

1. a theme's `add_node`, then the moves that put features under it, in their order;
2. a feature that stands alone, as a move with no `parent_id`, where a reader should meet it.

A feature you do not name stays above everything you do name.
