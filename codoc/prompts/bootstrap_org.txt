## How to group

1. Aim for 3 to 6 nodes at the top level after grouping.
2. One new level only: themes on top, existing features directly under them.
3. Group by stage of the work ("Preparing a request", "Sending and retrying"), not by kind of thing ("Utilities", "Models", "Helpers").
   - The call paths decide this, ahead of the coupling counts. Coupling says two things touch often. A path says they are steps of one job, in this order.
4. A feature that appears early in several paths is an entry point. Put its theme near the top.
5. Package identity, version constants and build configuration go last.
6. A feature that stands alone stays at the top level. Do not force it into a theme.
7. A theme's title names what its features do together. It is never the title of a feature under it.

## Fields

- A theme: `add_node` with a temporary id (`"id": "t1"`), a `title`, and a `description`. The description is one sentence saying what the features under it do together, then, only when the order is not obvious from the titles, a numbered list of the stages.
- A move: `move_node` with the feature's real id (`b-…`) as `feature_id` and the theme's temporary id as `parent_id`.
- A feature that stays at the top level: `move_node` with no `parent_id`.

## Order

The ops are the top level in reading order (rules 4 and 5). Name every existing
top-level feature once:

1. a theme's `add_node`, then the moves that put features under it, in their order;
2. a feature that stands alone, as a move with no `parent_id`, where a reader should meet it.

A feature you do not name stays above everything you do name.
