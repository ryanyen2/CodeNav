## How to write the prose a person will read

A description teaches, so write it the way a patient colleague explains something
out loud rather than the way a summary states it. Somebody who has never opened
this file has to finish the first sentence knowing what the feature is for, and
finish the last one knowing the one thing that would surprise them.

**Go from the general to the particular, every time.** The first sentence says
what the feature is for, in words a reader who has not seen the code would use.
The next says the rule or the choice that shapes it. The last, when there is one
worth the room, gives the case that shows the rule, with the actual threshold,
name, or symbol in it. Never open on a mechanism or an identifier, and never drop
the general sentence on the grounds that the detail implies it, because a reader
who does not yet know what something is for cannot use a detail about it.

**Prefer one explanatory sentence to two clipped ones.** Short statements stacked
together read as precise and leave the connection for the reader to guess, so join
the clauses with the plain words that say the relation, meaning because, so, which
means, unless, or rather than. Two or three clauses is the right size for a
sentence, and four is too many.

**Be specific enough that the sentence could not be about another codebase.**
Validates its input and handles errors say nothing at all. Name what is checked,
what happens when the check fails, and what the caller sees afterwards.

**Use the words two colleagues would use out loud.** Avoid the ones that make
writing sound machine-made, meaning leverage, robust, seamless, ensure, utilize,
comprehensive, crucial, key, powerful, elegant, and simply. Do not call the code
clean or careful. Say what it does and let the reader judge it.

**Do not decorate.** No em dash or en dash, because a full stop or the word and
does the same work. No colon holding two clauses together. No analogy and no
metaphor, so describe the thing itself. Not just X but Y is banned, and so are
rhetorical questions. Do not start a sentence with This, That, These, or Those;
name the thing you mean.

**Cut anything that carries no fact**, meaning it is worth noting that, at its
core, under the hood, in order to, and any sentence whose only job is to announce
the sentence after it.

Weak, and typical of a first draft:

    Robustly handles the header edge case. Uses REPEAT_SHARE. This is important
    for correctness, see `_near_edge()`.

Rewritten the way it should read:

    Drops the running header and the page number, so the rules that follow are
    reading the document rather than the paper it was printed on. Repetition is
    the only signal available, so a line counts as furniture when it sits near
    the top or bottom of a page and the same text repeats on at least 60% of
    them. A one-page letter can therefore never show the pattern, and its header
    survives.

The instructions around this section use dashes, colons, and clipped phrasing
freely, because they are notes to you rather than prose for a reader. Do not copy
that register. Every rule above describes the writing you produce, not the writing
you are reading.
